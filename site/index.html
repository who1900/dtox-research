Ask your agent

Find research on data availability sampling for blockchain light clients with limited bandwidth. Open a relevant paper, read its method and assumptions, and explain the implementation trade-offs with source citations. Distinguish the authors' findings from your own conclusions.

  1. Discover. Use find_papers or search_research_paper to find candidate sources.
  2. Inspect. Open a result with get_paper, then read the relevant method with read_paper_section.
  3. Check the detail. Retrieve equations or algorithms with get_code_or_math_spec, or collect a cited packet with get_research_bundle.

A quick starting point: ask for paper 2005.11401, Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks, then read its Introduction. Its card, outline and section read were checked through the public MCP on 17 October 2026.

Look for the source link, the actual passage and a clear connection between the evidence and the agent's conclusion. See the research workflow.

What you can do

From a promising abstract to an engineering decision.

Useful knowledge is scattered across papers, specifications and technical documents. An implementation often depends on an equation, a security assumption or a limitation buried in a later section. dtox brings those sources into the agent's workflow.

Read the method

Open source sections, equations, algorithms, code and tables where available. Inspect the evidence behind the answer.

Compare approaches

Explore related work, follow citation links and compare two methods' reported results and assumptions.

Investigate an idea

Pass distinct technical claims to validate_project. Review the candidate papers and read the passages supporting each claim.

Another way to try it

Use validate_project to investigate a retrieval-augmented language model. Evaluate this claim separately: "Retrieval augmented generation conditions language-model answers on retrieved documents." Show the candidate papers and supporting passages, then explain what still needs verification.

Use dtox to investigate data availability, account abstraction, protocol incentives and agent research systems. Responses identify the source and available coverage, including full text and abstract-only records.

Why Solana

Solana devnet integration

Wallet-signed readings · SAS

An agent signs a canonical message containing the claim, paper and verdict. The attestor verifies the reviewer's signature and records the reading through Solana Attestation Service. The signature identifies who signed; the source evidence supports the reading.

Explore the attestor

Agent payments · x402

A separate MCP gateway implements per-call USDC payments for research tools and signed readings on Solana devnet. Builders can explore this integration alongside the free research endpoint.

How it works

Find the paper. Retrieve the passage. Follow the source.

Semantic retrieval

Paper-level search shortlists relevant documents, then passage-level search finds the useful detail within their contents.

Exact technical terms

Lexical search adds precise terms, identifiers and protocol names to the semantic retrieval channels.

Citation links

Citation neighbours provide another route to related work. Results carry source links and metadata for inspection.

Built with FastAPI, Qdrant, SQLite FTS5 and ONNX embeddings, exposed through MCP. Read the architecture overview.
